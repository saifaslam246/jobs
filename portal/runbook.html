Connecting your email, step by step

Google will not let any program send from your account with your normal password. It needs a separate 16-character App Password, which only exists once 2-Step Verification is on. That is the whole reason this takes four steps instead of one.

  1. Turn on 2-Step Verification — do this first Go to myaccount.google.com/signinoptions/twosv and switch it on if it is not already. Without it the App Passwords page does not exist, and you will get “the setting you are looking for is not available”.
  2. Create the App Password Go to myaccount.google.com/apppasswords. Type any name — jobs pipeline — and press Create. Google shows 16 characters in four groups, like abcd efgh ijkl mnop. Copy it now; it is never shown again. Spaces are fine, they get stripped.
  3. Add five secrets to the repository Go to github.com/saifaslam246/jobs → Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions. Press New repository secret, fill in Name and Secret, press Add secret — then repeat for each row below. Five separate secrets, not one.
  4. Test it before it touches a real company Go to the Test email setup workflow → Run workflow. It sends exactly one email, to you, and nothing else. A green tick and a message in your inbox means it is done. A red cross tells you which of the three usual mistakes it was.

Questions you have already asked

Does it work when I close Claude?

Yes. The harvest and the sending run on GitHub's machines. The 06:30 job is a brand new Claude session that starts on a schedule, does the work, and shuts down. Nothing needs this conversation, or your laptop.

Can it send without me approving at all?

Yes — one word in outreach/config.json, approve_first to auto. In auto mode a draft only leaves if it scores 60+, has a real contact address, has no eligibility flag, and has a CV attached. Anything failing one of those waits for you instead of being dropped.

Where do the jobs live?

Two places. The full archive of every scored role sits in the repository, which is what the automation reads. The top 100 plus your status on each sit in the portal's own database, which is what you look at.

Why is LinkedIn not in there?

It has no public job API. The only way to pull it is to drive your logged-in session with your own cookie, which breaks their terms and gets accounts restricted. Your LinkedIn is worth more than the extra listings.

My portal is empty.

Look at the strip under the tabs. Amber with an “Allow access” button means the page has not been given permission to read its own database yet — tap it.
